Expansion Explorer

How does the size of the universe, a(t), change over time? Choose today’s expansion rate H₀ and how much matter (Ωm) and vacuum energy (ΩΛ) the universe holds. Every curve passes through today at a = 1 with slope ȧ = H₀; what the universe contains decides how its scale factor evolves. Below the plot, the moment slider (or a click on the plot) picks a moment in time; the pie chart on the plot shows what your universe is made of at that moment. The tick boxes under the plot switch the axes to log scales, and add the most distant galaxy and SNe Ia observed. On a log time axis our own universe also shades important epochs in its evolution.

Radiation (photons and neutrinos) dominated only for the first ~50,000 years, before z ≈ 3400; to see it, tick “Include radiation” and type a large redshift. Ω values are fractions of the critical density at that moment. Flat universes have a total of exactly 1. For curved ones the total misses 1, and the gap is written Ωk = 1 − (total): positive for open universes (total below 1), negative for closed ones (total above 1). Ωk is not a substance, just bookkeeping for the curvature.

How this works

The size of the universe is a(t), with a = 1 today. Its rate of growth is ȧ (like a velocity), and how fast that rate changes is ä (like an acceleration). Matter slows the expansion; vacuum energy, with pressure p = −ρc², speeds it up:

ä / a = −(4πG/3)(ρ + 3p/c²)  ⟹  ä = H₀² a (ΩΛ − Ωm / (2a³) − Ω_rad / a⁴)

The program starts today with a = 1 and ȧ = H₀, then steps this equation backward and forward in time. Going back, a either reaches zero (a Big Bang) or stops shrinking and turns around (no Big Bang). The empty universe is a straight line, so its age is exactly 1/H₀. Radiation (Ω_rad = 4.15 × 10⁻⁵ / h², from the measured CMB temperature plus neutrinos) is included unless you switch it off; it adds a term −Ω_rad/a⁴ inside the bracket and matters only in the first few hundred thousand years.

Composition at a moment. The moment slider picks a time. For each universe on the plot, the program reads off its size a at that time and works out each ingredient's share of the critical density: matter thins out as 1/a³, radiation as 1/a⁴, vacuum energy stays the same, and the curvature share as 1/a². Typing a redshift instead picks the moment when a = 1/(1 + z), which comes at a different time in each universe.

H₀ in km/s per Mpc converts to a growth rate per billion years: 1 km/s/Mpc = 1/977.8 per billion years, so H₀ = 70 means a grows by about 7% of today's size per billion years.
